# Why the site reads "too corporate" — audit, 5 Aug 2026

Mark's objection was "too corporate / markety". I tested that against the actual copy.

## It is NOT buzzwords

Swept every page for the usual offenders — unlock, empower, seamless, leverage,
robust, holistic, cutting-edge, transform, elevate, streamline, world-class, journey,
solutions, innovative. **Three hits across the entire site**, and two are false
positives ("The Journey" as a section label, "elevated work platforms" — MEWPs
genuinely are that).

The copy is already concrete. It names JCT, NEC, novation, retentions, payless
notices, CDM, gross margin, stepping out of the van, the £1m–£2m ceiling. That is
not generic marketing language and it should not be thrown away.

## It IS that Mark never speaks

This is the finding.

**There is not one sentence of Mark talking anywhere on the site.**

His own founder page describes him in the third person, like a company boilerplate:

> "Over 25 years, Mark Poulton started with a single M&E firm and built it into a
> substantial contracting operation."
> "He has priced the jobs, signed the contracts, carried payroll…"
> "This isn't someone who read about your industry."

That is a press release about a man. It is not the man. A reader arrives wanting to
judge whether they'd take advice from him, and he never opens his mouth.

Person-count across the site confirms it:

| Page | Mark in 3rd person | 1st person | "you" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | 2 | 2 | 20 |
| Academy | 4 | 2 | 11 |
| Our Story | 1 | 2 | **1** |
| Mentorship/Pricing/etc | 7 | 5 | 8 |

The homepage talks *to* the customer well — 20 uses of "you". But **Our Story, the
page whose entire job is Mark, uses "you" once and lets him say nothing.**

## Why this reads as corporate

Third-person founder copy is the house style of every company that has a marketing
department and no founder worth quoting. It is the single strongest signal that a
business is speaking through an agency. Mark has the opposite problem: he has 25
years of things worth saying and none of them are on the page.

## What fixing it requires

Not a rewrite of the arguments — they are sound. A rewrite of **who is talking**.

- Our Story becomes Mark in first person, with at least two verbatim passages.
- The homepage founder section carries a real quote, not a description.
- The Academy page carries his view on why each module exists, in his words.

**This cannot be done without hearing him.** Writing "Mark's voice" from imagination
is exactly the thing that produces AI-sounding copy, and it would be dishonest to put
invented sentences in a real person's mouth.

## What is needed, in order of value

1. **Transcripts of the 6 course videos** (21 minutes of Mark explaining business
   concepts). Richest source by far — I found the videos on Wistia but no captions
   exist and I have no transcription tool in this session.
2. **A 10-minute voice note** answering the questions below. Unscripted and rambling
   is ideal — sentence patterns matter more than polish.
3. Written samples — emails, LinkedIn posts, messages to clients or the team.

## Questions for Mark (for the voice note)

Designed to produce quotable copy, not biography. He should talk, not write.

1. Tell me about a job you priced wrong. What happened, and what did it cost you?
2. An owner says "we're busy all year but there's nothing left at the end of it."
   What do you say back?
3. Which contract clause has cost you the most money, and how did you find out?
4. The pre-pack — what actually happened, and what would you say to someone who can
   see it coming?
5. What do most engineering business owners get wrong without realising it?
6. When you first stepped out of the van, what broke?
7. What annoys you about business courses and consultants?

Question 7 matters more than it looks. What someone is *against* reveals their voice
faster than what they are for.
